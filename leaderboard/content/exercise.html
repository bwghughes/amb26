    Mac Initiative Builder

    Build a Mac app by talking to it — three jobs, a look of your own, forty-five minutes, no code, and the customer's words never leave the laptop.

    What you're building

    An app that records a sales conversation, pulls out the key points, and drafts the follow-up email — all on this MacBook, with the Wi-Fi off.

    You won't write any code. You'll describe what you want and Xcode's assistant will build it. Say each sentence out loud if you can — the dictation is on-device too, which is the same thing you're selling. Every pair's window should look different on the gallery wall; this Mac already has a seed (title, colour, a customer in a particular industry). At the end you'll push that further.

    How this guide works

    1. One step per screen. Work through it, tick the boxes, then press Next.
    2. Arrow keys ← → also move between steps.
    3. Stuck at any point? If it goes wrong, top right — it brings you back where you were.
    4. Start the clock in the top bar when your facilitator says go.
    5. If you're on the contest website, keep this tab open. You'll turn Wi-Fi off later; the steps stay here.

    Set up 0:00 – 0:04

    Four minutes. Get the half-built app running, then wake up the assistant.

    1. On the Desktop, open the Starter folder and double-click Ambassadors26.xcodeproj. Xcode opens.
    2. Click the ▶ Run button, top-left (or press ⌘R). Wait for the app window to appear.
    3. In the app, click Use sample. Notes from a fake customer fill the left pane. The window title and colour on this Mac may not match the projector — that's your pair's seed, on purpose.
    4. Click Build initiative. Nothing happens. That's your job today.
    5. Leave the app running. Back in Xcode, open the Coding Assistant panel. (Your facilitator will show you where it is.)
    6. Paste the sentence below into the assistant and press ↩. It should reply ready. Skip this if it already said it is following AGENTS.md.
    Paste this once — type it, don't dictate it

    Read AGENTS.md, Ambassadors26/BUILD-SPEC.md, and SEED.md. Follow them whenever I ask for something, and prefer the exact APIs they name. Reply with just “ready”.

    This points the assistant at the project's build notes, so everything after this can be plain English.

    Before you move on

    1 Pull out the key points 0:04 – 0:16

    Right now the button does nothing. One sentence from you and it'll read the notes and give you fields you could drop into a CRM.

    Say this

    There are notes from a sales call in the left-hand pane. Make the Build initiative button actually read them and give me proper fields I could drop straight into our CRM, with how urgent this customer is right at the top — and all of it has to happen on this Mac, nothing leaves the laptop.

    Then, click by click

    1. Wait while it works. It will write several files — a minute or two is normal.
    2. When it's finished, glance at what it changed. You're not checking the code, only that it did what you asked.
    3. Press ▶ Run again (⌘R).

    4. Click Use sample, then Build initiative.
    5. Watch for the spinner and Analyzing…, then a coloured urgency badge and six labelled fields in the middle pane.
    Now the moment worth remembering. Click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar and turn Wi-Fi off completely. Change a word in the notes. Click Build initiative again. It still works — because the model is on this laptop, not in somebody else's cloud.

    Before you move on

    Why that worked 0:43 – 0:45

    Your sentences were short, but the app still came out right. That's not mind-reading — there's a file in the project called BUILD-SPEC.md (and AGENTS.md) holding the detail you didn't say: which Apple frameworks to use, that it must never call the internet, and which of two similar-looking speech technologies is the current one.

    Somebody wrote that file, once. Write the brief down properly a single time, and from then on everyone can ask for what they want in plain English. The seed and the last sentence are the same idea: same spec, different personality.

    The on-device model is small and fast. It's excellent at pulling structure out of messy notes. It will not write your account strategy. Don't sell it as more than that.

    Two more sentences to try later are in STRETCH.md. Facilitators: see facilitator notes.

    If it goes wrong

    All of this is normal. Say the fix out loud, the same as everything else — you don't need to understand the error.

    What you're seeingWhat to say
    Xcode shows a red errorCopy the error text, paste it in, and add fix this
    It built something oddHave another look at BUILD-SPEC.md — that isn't what it asks for.
    It asked you for an API key or an accountNo. This has to run on the Mac, on device. No network calls.
    Something that used to work has stoppedYou've broken something that was working — put it back.
    The button does nothing at allI clicked Build initiative and nothing happened.
    Two attempts have failedIn Terminal, from the Starter folder: git checkout . — that rewinds to the start. Then say the sentence again.
    A notice about Apple Intelligence being unavailableNot your fault and not fixable here. Put your hand up.
    Dictation garbled your sentenceUse the Copy button instead and move on. Don't lose two minutes to it.
    It used the old speech API / words only appear after you stopHave another look at BUILD-SPEC.md — section 3. Use SpeechAnalyzer, not SFSpeechRecognizer. I want the words to appear while I'm still talking.
    The site says that team name is already inThat's fine if it's yours — use the same name and PIN to resume. Otherwise pick a different name.
